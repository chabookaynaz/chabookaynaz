# Gate Valve Body Casting Design

**English companion to the Persian design report**  
Aynaz Chabouk | Individual coursework | Principles of Solidification and Casting

## Project overview
This project proposes a sand-casting route for a gate valve body. It connects component function and geometry to alloy selection, mold and core design, feeding and gating calculations, quality control and finishing. It is a design study; the submitted material does not document manufacture or testing of a finished valve.

## Component function and casting challenges
The body contains the flow passage and accommodates the valve mechanism while forming a pressure-containing enclosure. The report discusses applications in oil and gas, thermal power and chemical processing. Its casting analysis identifies variable section thickness, heavy flange regions and an internal cavity as key challenges. The thicker regions are treated as potential hot spots and shrinkage locations.

## Material and proposed manufacturing route
The selected material is cast carbon steel designated ASTM A216 Grade WCB in the source report. The proposed route uses a rigid resin-bonded sand mold, described in the coursework as a Cold Box route, with silica and chromite sands. The report compares this choice with die casting, investment casting and green-sand molding in terms of geometry, temperature, gas entrapment, surface quality and cost.

The pattern is split through the horizontal axis of the body. Wood is discussed for lower production quantities and aluminum for higher quantities. The source proposes approximately 1.8-2% shrinkage allowance and 0.5-1.5° draft, together with machining allowances and core prints.

The sacrificial sand core forms the intersecting internal passages. The design discusses a ceramic core coating, vent passages and support through core prints. It proposes avoiding chaplets where the supports can adequately restrain the core.

## Geometry and calculation sequence
The simplified calculation model uses a 500 mm body length, a 240 mm outer body diameter, a 200 mm bore, 400 mm flange outer diameters and a 30 mm flange axial thickness. The source estimates a total casting volume of approximately 13,735 cm³ and a mass of 107.8 kg using a steel density of 7.85 g/cm³.

### Feeding-system study
The report applies a modulus criterion of Mr ≥ 1.2 Mc and evaluates cylindrical risers with H = 1.5D. It also checks liquid-metal volume requirements using 3% solidification shrinkage and an assumed feeding efficiency of 14%.

The source obtains a total required riser volume of approximately 3,746 cm³. It then considers three larger risers, approximately 102 mm in diameter and 153 mm high, and discusses chills opposite the risers to address feeding distance. An alternative calculation uses exothermic assistance and an efficiency of 0.67, producing a total riser volume of approximately 644 cm³ and individual dimensions near D = 57 mm and H = 85.5 mm.

**Reading note:** these are reported coursework calculations. The source prints the alternative efficiency as “0.67%” but substitutes 0.67 in its equation. It also uses the 80 mm radial width of a flange in a feeding-distance expression, whereas the listed axial thickness is 30 mm. These assumptions and the cooling-area calculation need reconciliation before the dimensions are reused. The English companion preserves the reported results without presenting them as validated design values.

### Gating-system study
For a horizontal mold orientation, the report assumes a 45 cm total head and calculates an effective head of 40 cm. It estimates a pouring time of 18.5 seconds, a discharge coefficient of 0.45 and a choke area of approximately 5.89 cm².

The proposed non-pressurized gating ratio is 1:2:2, giving a runner area and a combined ingate area of approximately 11.78 cm² each. Two ingates are proposed to distribute entry into the cavity while limiting the number of gate-removal locations.

## Inspection and finishing plan
The report proposes dimensional inspection, visual inspection, liquid penetrant testing, radiographic examination and hydrostatic testing. It discusses dimensional allowances with reference to ISO 8062-3 and identifies MSS SP-55, ASTM E165 and ASTM E446 in its inspection discussion. These references describe the coursework's proposed inspection plan, not completed inspections or verified compliance.

The finishing sequence includes removal of gates and risers, surface cleaning, heat treatment and machining of functional surfaces. Normalizing and shot blasting are discussed as part of the proposed route.

## Main takeaway
The project demonstrates an integrated casting-design workflow, including recognition of hot spots and an attempt to balance thermal and volumetric feeding requirements. Its deliverables are a proposed process and hand calculations, rather than a manufactured or experimentally validated casting.
